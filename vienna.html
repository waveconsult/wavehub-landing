Khachanov against Alcaraz

Erste Bank Open, Vienna · first round

Everyone is looking at the wrong market here. The question is not whether Khachanov can win. It is whether he can stay close.

The call
Khachanov +4.5 games

Not Khachanov to win at 4.50. He has never beaten Alcaraz in six attempts, and that price is asking you to bet on the one thing that has never happened.

Alcaraz is a different player under a roof

His career win rate tells the whole story. On grass he wins 88.1 percent of his matches, on clay 81.1, on outdoor hard 78.7. Indoors it falls to 70.6.

That is still a good number for most players. For someone who lives above 80 everywhere else, it is a hole in the wall. And this is his first indoor match of the season, so he has no rhythm in these conditions yet.

Khachanov's serve is built for this room

Indoors there is no wind and no sun, so a flat, heavy serve behaves the same way every single time. That is exactly what he has. He lands around 63 percent of first serves and wins three quarters of those points, averages 7.1 aces a match, and when he does get into trouble he saves 64 percent of the break points he faces.

Vienna is not a strange building for him either. He has a 12 and 9 record at this tournament, reached the final in 2024, and four of his career titles have come on indoor hard.

The maths of plus 4.5

Over the last 52 weeks on hard courts both men have held 88 percent of their service games. If Khachanov holds anywhere near that rate, Alcaraz gets one or two breaks in the entire match, and one or two breaks is not enough to cover a 4.5 game line.

Run the realistic scripts. Alcaraz wins 6-4, 6-4 and the bet still cashes. He wins 7-5, 6-4 and it cashes. Khachanov takes a single set and it is home long before the end. The only way it loses is a blowout.

Their history backs it up. Alcaraz leads the head to head 6-0, but the recent meetings have been tight. In Doha, Khachanov took the first set before losing in three. In Rome in 2025 it went to three sets as well. Both covered a plus 4.5 line comfortably.

Where it goes wrong

Khachanov played deep into Almaty last week and is on short rest. If his legs go, his first serve percentage drops, and Alcaraz punishes weak second serves better than anyone on tour, winning 54 percent of those points. One tired 6-2 set and the cushion is gone, even if the other set is a tight 6-4.

That is the whole risk. Not that Khachanov is outclassed, but that he runs out of fuel in one stretch.

What the price should be

The handicap sits around 1.60 to 1.67 in most books. Anything at 1.65 or better is a fair grab. Below 1.55 you are paying too much for the safety net and the edge is gone.

For comparison, Alcaraz on the moneyline is around 1.16 to 1.20. There is nothing there. The value in this match lives on the handicap, not on the winner.
